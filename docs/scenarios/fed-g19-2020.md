# March 2020 Federal Reserve G.19 revolving-credit boundary replay

This counted scenario places a historical decision boundary at 2020-04-07 19:00 UTC, the exact
3:00 p.m. EDT time printed on the archived April 7 Federal Reserve G.19 Consumer Credit release.
Its two inputs are the January revised and February preliminary revolving-credit simple annual
rates in that one release snapshot. The May 7 March value is locked separately as a post-decision
event and is absent from every ReplayPack source record.

The source reports aggregate credit outstanding and changes. Revolving credit includes most
credit-card loans but also other revolving plans. This case does not claim that FinReplay existed
at the historical boundary, observe spending, households, card accounts, transactions, lenders,
positions, or orders, identify a consumer, pandemic, policy, lender, or macroeconomic mechanism,
or predict a future release.

## Official releases and knowledge boundary

`scenarios/fed-g19-2020/input-lock.json` contains exactly two reported facts from the April 7
release:

- January 2020: revised revolving-credit simple annual rate `-2.7%`, or `-270` basis points;
  the adapter's release-history bridge preserves the previous March 6 snapshot's preliminary
  `-3.3%` value and the resulting `+60`-basis-point revision;
- February 2020: preliminary revolving-credit simple annual rate `4.6%`, or `460` basis points.

Both records share the April PDF's SHA-256 and exact availability time. The adapter requires every
selected release to be an official four-page `612 x 792` PDF with all pages rotated 90 degrees and
a nonblank text layer. It validates release identity, stated 3:00 p.m. EST/EDT time, the seasonally
adjusted simple-annual-rate table, all retained total/revolving/nonrevolving changes, flows and
outstanding levels, the unrounded-data footnote, and `r`/`p` estimate markers. The timezone
abbreviation is checked under `America/New_York`; current HTTP metadata is not backdated.

G.19 says the simple annual rates are calculated from unrounded data. The adapter therefore keeps
the table's one-decimal values and does not substitute the release headline's rounded fractions.
The two locked values are versioned release-snapshot facts; later revisions cannot overwrite them.

## Transparent range with no probability

ShockCompiler uses only the two April 7 table values:

- latest-known persistence baseline: `460` basis points;
- one known January-to-February increase: `460 - (-270) = 730` basis points;
- stress endpoints: persistence at `460`, or one repeat of the increase at `1,190` basis points;
- range width: `730` basis points;
- probability assigned: none.

This is a deliberately transparent stress construction from two values and one difference. It is
not a forecast, confidence interval, calibrated coverage band, stationary-regime estimate, or
causal model. Neither the May release nor any later revision helps choose or widen the endpoints.

## Disjoint post-decision event and revisions

`scenarios/fed-g19-2020/event-lock.json` contains the May 7 release's preliminary March 2020
revolving-credit simple annual rate of `-30.9%`, or `-3,090` basis points. Its stated 3:00 p.m. EDT
release time is 2020-05-07 19:00 UTC, strictly after the decision boundary. The event record ID is
disjoint from both input IDs.

The same May release snapshot records January at `-370` basis points and February at `360` basis
points, each `100` basis points below its April decision-snapshot value. The event record retains
both old and revised values and their arithmetic deltas. The input lock remains January `-270` and
February `460`; no later value overwrites the historical view.

The reported March value is `3,550` basis points below the previously declared lower endpoint:
`460 - (-3,090) = 3,550`. The verifier requires this miss to remain visible. It does not widen the
range, relabel the outcome as success, or treat one failure as a calibrated performance estimate.

## Four relevant engines

TimeVault reconstructs the one-release, two-record decision set; ShockCompiler compiles the
no-probability persistence-or-one-known-increase endpoints; TrialCourt retains and rejects a
retrospective one-increase attempt; ReplayStudio exports a deterministic human- and
machine-readable pack. MarketTwin, ExecutionLab, and CapitalAllocator are absent because no
household or institutional network, security, order, execution, portfolio, allocation, capital,
or return evidence exists for this question.
